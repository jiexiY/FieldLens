THE IDEA

More context.
On your terms.

A map can show where you’re going. RealLens helps explain the environment around that journey.

Public information is scattered across forecast pages, construction notices, transit updates, and satellite datasets. RealLens gathers a focused set of those sources and translates them into plain-language context—without requiring you to interpret a map.

The goal is preparation, not instructions about how someone should travel. Travelers keep control of their plans, their microphone, and whether to listen or read.

PUBLIC DATA, WITH CONTEXT

Every source
has a story.
And a limit.

A forecast is not a puddle sensor. A satellite image is not a sidewalk inspection.

RealLens keeps retrieval times, source dates, and uncertainty attached to its information. Fast checking does not make every source live, complete, or locally precise.

FORECAST

National Weather Service

Hourly rain probability, air temperature, wind, and current alerts for the checked campus area. The briefing uses your travel time window; weather refreshes when you prepare a briefing.

An area forecast cannot identify slippery pavement, puddles, or conditions at a particular crossing.

PUBLISHED NOTICES

UF Campus Closures

Public campus notices and official closure polygons provide context for a prepared journey. Notices are checked every 15 seconds while the page is visible and online.

UF must publish first. Short caches and network delays apply. A missing or removed notice is not proof that a walkway is open.

RIDER ALERTS

Gainesville RTS

Published bus posts, route filtering, source dates, and optional narration. An independent monitor checks for changes every 15 seconds while the app is visible and online.

Not live bus positions or arrival predictions. No background push after closing the page. Images in RTS posts are not interpreted.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT

Sentinel-2 + EMERGE

September 22, 2024 satellite data describes vegetation signal along the mapped study line. The method adapts EMERGE Textbook 1, Chapter 3, Lesson 3: Vegetation & Water Indices: a way to turn public environmental evidence into understandable context.

Historical vegetation is not current shade, surface condition, or an accessibility rating. The map uses attributed OpenStreetMap geometry, not a verified accessible route.

EMERGE method ↗

Track method: NDVI = (near-infrared − red) / (near-infrared + red). We adapt the lesson to one dated Sentinel-2 scene, not its temporal median. GLOBE Observer and geoemerge are not used. See the method, datasets, and reproduction steps.

A SMALLER INTERFACE. A CLEARER START.

Four choices.
Room to breathe.

A white welcome screen leads to four large actions. Each action opens its own page. No promotional paragraphs, no moving carousel, and no microphone that turns itself on.

Inside the app, typed fallback, keyboard controls, larger text, higher contrast, and optional spoken briefings support different preferences. Essential journey information does not require map interaction.

WHERE THE PROTOTYPE STANDS

Preparation.
Not navigation.

The walking pilot covers six places: Reitz Union, Marston Science Library, Turlington Hall, Smathers Library, The Hub, and Newell Hall. It is not a Gainesville-wide routing service.

RealLens cannot detect obstacles, verify entrance access, or certify a safe or accessible route. It does not replace a traveler’s mobility tools or orientation-and-mobility support.

It has not yet been evaluated with blind or low-vision participants. Automated checks are not proof of usability or safety. Co-design and real assistive-technology testing remain necessary next steps.
